THE MIRROR SEDENIONS:
A SECOND G2-SYMMETRIC DOUBLING OF THE OCTONIONS
AND THE GEOMETRY OF ITS ZERO DIVISORS
LUI
Abstract. The Cayley–Dickson double of the octonions may be formed with the product of
the founding copy of O written backwards:
(a, b)(c, d) = (ca −d b, da + b c).
For a commutative base this changes nothing; over H it already produces the quasi-octonions
instead of O; over O it produces a 16-dimensional real algebra S′, the mirror sedenions, which is
flexible, quadratic, carries the diagonal action of G2, and is not isomorphic to the sedenions S.
We prove that S′ is the subalgebra O + O (ee′) of the 32-dimensional Cayley–Dickson algebra
spanned by O and the composite unit ee′ (the algebra noticed but not studied by Cawagas et
al.), that Bales’s eight admissible doubling products applied to O yield exactly the two algebras
S and S′, and we determine the structure of S′ completely. The stretch operator L¯xLx has the
five-level spectrum N(x) ± 2| Im a| |b|, N(x) ± 2| Im a| |b∥|, N(x) with multiplicities 2, 2, 4, 4, 4,
where x = a + bℓand b∥is the projection of b to the complex line R + R Im a. Consequently
the norm-one zero divisors of S′ form the product of round spheres S6 × S7 (a 13-manifold,
against the Stiefel manifold V2(R7) of Moreno and Biss–Dugger–Isaksen for S), annihilators are
2-dimensional except on a 7-dimensional locus where they are 6-dimensional, and the manifold
of norm-one zero-divisor pairs is diffeomorphic to V2(R7) × S3, the trivial S3-bundle over the
Stiefel manifold, whereas for S it is G2, the principal SU(2)-bundle over the same base. The
eight octonion subalgebras spanned by basis hyperplanes are the eight hyperplanes of PG(3, 2)
avoiding the doubling point, whereas for S they are the founding octave and the seven hyperplanes
through the doubling point; the seven remaining hyperplanes are quasi-octonion algebras in both
cases. Aut(S′) = G2 × Z/2, in contrast with Brown’s G2 × S3 for S. The difference between the
two algebras is a single sign function χ on F4
2 × F4
2 which is not a 2-cocycle: the “orientation bit”
of the fourth doubling.

1. Introduction
Let O be the real octonions with conjugation x 7→¯x and norm N(x) = x¯x. The Cayley–Dickson
double of O is the sedenion algebra S = O ⊕Oℓwith
(1)
(a + bℓ)(c + dℓ) = (ac −¯db) + (da + b¯c)ℓ.
The literature contains many conventions for this formula. Bales [2] lists eight “doubling products”
that yield a Cayley–Dickson algebra when applied at every level starting from R, and notes that
the four “transposed” products give the opposite algebras of the other four. The present paper
begins with a different question: what happens when one of these eight products is applied once,
on top of the standard octonions? The answer is that exactly two algebras arise. Four of Bales’s
products give S. The other four give the algebra
(2)
(a + bℓ)(c + dℓ) = (ca −¯db) + (da + b¯c)ℓ,
which differs from (1) in a single slot: the product of the two octonions from the founding copy
of O is taken backwards. We call this algebra the mirror sedenions and denote it S′. For any
base algebra A we write M(A) for the algebra A ⊕A with product (2) and call it the mirror
double of A; thus S′ = M(O).
If A is commutative, M(A) = CD(A). If A is associative but not commutative the two doubles
are already different: M(H) is not a composition algebra. Over the octonions the change of
a single letter produces a genuinely new object with a rich and very clean geometry, which is
the subject of this paper. Our main results, all proved by hand and independently verified by
computer, are the following. Throughout, x = a + bℓ∈S′ with a, b ∈O, N(x) = |a|2 + |b|2, and
b∥denotes the orthogonal projection of b onto the complex line Ca = R + R Im a.
(A) Embedding (Theorem 3.3).
For every unital ∗-algebra A, the mirror double M(A) is
isomorphic to the subalgebra A + A (ee′) of the double double CD(CD(A)) spanned by
A and the product ee′ of the two doubling units, via a + bℓ7→¯a + (be)e′. Hence M(H)
is the quasi-octonion algebra H + H(εℓ) ⊂S (ε ⊥H a unit octonion) of Cawagas [8]
and Chan–¯Dokovi´c [10], and S′ is the 16-dimensional subalgebra Sγ = O + O(ee′) of the
trigintaduonions which Cawagas et al. [9] found to be the unique basis hyperplane of T with
eight octonion subalgebras not isomorphic to S.

b in the chart H(u, n), and the bundle is trivial. Moreno’s theorem that the zero-divisor pairs of
S form a G2-torsor [18] is, in this light, a statement about the alignment of the orientation of the
founding octave with the doubling twist, not about G2 alone: S′ has the same G2 and no torsor.

algebra (its dimension-8 stage is already M(H) rather than O, and its dimension-16 stage has a
6-dimensional derivation algebra; Remark 3.8). The algebra S′ itself appears, as the subalgebra
Sγ ⊂T, in the loop-theoretic census of Cawagas et al. [9], who record that it has eight octonion
and seven quasi-octonion subloops and is not isomorphic to S, and prove nothing further. The
zero-divisor theory of S is due to Moreno [18], Biss–Dugger–Isaksen [3, 4] and Biss–Christensen–
Dugger–Isaksen [5]; its Riemannian geometry to Reggiani [19]; the automorphism group to Brown
[7] (see also Eakin–Sathaye [13]); the derivation algebra to Schafer [22]; the finite geometry of
its multiplication table to Saniga–Holweck–Pracna [21], Cawagas [8] and de Marrais [11]; the
twisted-group-algebra viewpoint to Albuquerque–Majid [1]. We found no prior description of the
zero divisors, annihilators, spectrum, automorphisms, or subalgebra geometry of S′, and no prior
formulation of the mirror double as a construction.

2. Conventions and octonion facts
2.1. Cayley–Dickson algebras. A ∗-algebra is a unital real algebra A with a linear involution
x 7→¯x satisfying xy = ¯y¯x and ¯1 = 1. Its Cayley–Dickson double is CD(A) = A ⊕Ae with
the product (1) (with ℓreplaced by e) and involution a + be = ¯a −be. Starting from R with
the trivial involution one obtains C, H, O, S, T, . . . ; we write O = CD(H) with doubling unit e4,
S = CD(O) with doubling unit ℓ= e8, and T = CD(S) with doubling unit e′ = e16.
Convention 2.1. The basis of O is e0 = 1, e1, . . . , e7 with egeh = ±eg⊕h (⊕= bitwise XOR), e1
the imaginary unit of C, e2 the doubling unit of H, e4 that of O. With (1) the oriented lines of
the Fano plane are

via a 7→¯a when an algebra isomorphism is needed, and otherwise simply regard a, b in x = a + bℓ

2.2. Octonion identities. We use freely: O is alternative, so any two elements generate an
associative subalgebra (Artin) which is contained in a quaternion subalgebra; the Moufang

y ∈Im O and y ⊥u.

2.3. Facts about G2. G2 = Aut(O) acts on Im O ∼= R7 preserving the inner product and the
cross product. It acts transitively on the unit sphere S6 with stabilizer SU(3), transitively on the
Stiefel manifold V2(R7) of orthonormal pairs in Im O with stabilizer SU(2), and transitively on
the set GrH(O) ∼= G2/SO(4) of quaternion subalgebras [14, 20]. The stabilizer of an orthonormal
pair (u, n) fixes the quaternion algebra H(u, n) = R + Ru + Rn + R un pointwise. Given a, b ∈O
there is g ∈G2 with ga ∈R + Re1 and gb ∈R + Re1 + Re2; we call this the normal form of the
pair.

satisfies x2 −2t(x)x + N(x) = 0 with t(x), N(x) ∈R, and flexible if (xy)x = x(yx). In a flexible
quadratic algebra with ¯x = 2t(x) −x one has
