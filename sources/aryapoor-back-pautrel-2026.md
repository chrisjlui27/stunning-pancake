# Aryapoor--Bäck--Pautrel 2026 --- involutions in the CD construction

## Why this source matters

**The highest-risk item found.** It classifies exactly what Theorem 3.6 classifies, and it is from this year.

## Claims taken from it (abstract only)

- Determines **all involutions in the Cayley--Dickson construction that extend the involution of the original \*-algebra**.
- Finds **all algebra isomorphisms between the resulting Cayley doubles that extend the identity automorphism** of the original \*-algebra.
- **Consequently classifies the resulting \*-algebras up to \*-algebra isomorphism.**
- Applications: Cayley doubles without zero divisors admit exactly one additional involution; the classical CD involution is the unique scalar involution; classification of \*-algebras arising from R up to dimension 4.

## Risk to the paper

**High, on the face of it.** Theorem 3.6 says: applied to O, Bales's eight products give exactly two algebras, S and S′, with explicit isomorphisms of the form a + bl -> a^(bar) + (+/- b^(bar))l. Those isomorphisms are precisely 'algebra isomorphisms between Cayley doubles extending the identity automorphism of the base'. If this paper's classification covers the doubling variants rather than only the involution variants, part (B) may be a special case of it.

Two reasons it may not bite: (i) its stated applications stop at **dimension 4**, well short of the 16-dimensional case; (ii) it is framed around *involutions* on a fixed doubling product, whereas Theorem 3.6 varies the *product*. Those are different axes. But 'probably a different axis' is not a resolution.
